breathing, sensed without a camera.

Saans (साँस, “breath”) turns the WiFi signal already crossing a room into presence, breathing and safety signals. No camera, no wearable, no microphone. The raw radio never leaves the sensor.

the problem

Cameras see too much. Motion sensors see too little. A quiet room full of readers looks empty to a PIR, and a camera in a hostel corridor is a liability.

cameras, PIR and wearables

  • Cameras capture faces, bodies and identity, all personal data under the DPDP Act from the first frame.
  • PIR needs movement. Someone reading or sleeping disappears.
  • Wearables need a willing person who remembers to charge and wear them.
  • Cameras need light, or IR, and a place where filming is acceptable.

Either they record you, or they guess.

saans

  • Radio amplitude and phase only. There is no image or audio anywhere in the system.
  • The breathing band picks up the still reader a PIR misses.
  • Nothing to wear, carry or charge. Nobody has to cooperate.
  • Works in the dark, because radio does not need light.

It measures a space, says how sure it is, and stays quiet when it can't tell.

Radio has limits too. It cannot tell who anyone is, a single link can go blind at the wrong spot, and none of this has been measured in a real room yet. Each of those limits is covered below.
