breathing, sensed without a camera.

Saans (साँस, “breath”) turns the WiFi signal already crossing a room into presence, breathing and safety signals. No camera, no wearable, no microphone. The raw radio never leaves the sensor.

the problem

Cameras see too much. Motion sensors see too little. A quiet room full of readers looks empty to a PIR, and a camera in a hostel corridor is a liability.

cameras, PIR and wearables

  • Cameras capture faces, bodies and identity, all personal data under the DPDP Act from the first frame.
  • PIR needs movement. Someone reading or sleeping disappears.
  • Wearables need a willing person who remembers to charge and wear them.
  • Cameras need light, or IR, and a place where filming is acceptable.

Either they record you, or they guess.

saans

  • Radio amplitude and phase only. There is no image or audio anywhere in the system.
  • The breathing band picks up the still reader a PIR misses.
  • Nothing to wear, carry or charge. Nobody has to cooperate.
  • Works in the dark, because radio does not need light.

It measures a space, says how sure it is, and stays quiet when it can't tell.

Radio has limits too. It cannot tell who anyone is, a single link can go blind at the wrong spot, and none of this has been measured in a real room yet. Each of those limits is covered below.

how it works

A dedicated transmitter gives the receiver a channel to measure. The receiver turns 20 channel snapshots a second into one 68-byte summary, twice a second.

The gateway fuses those links into a space and decides what to report, and when to stay silent.

tx nodeesp-now · 50 Hz beacon, 12 bytes
rx nodec++ · float32 · no heap capture CSI fill the gaps suppress the room fuse subcarriers presence breathing feature frame + consent gate → 68 B · 2 Hz · UDP
gatewayrust · f64 fuse links → detectors → events

The same stages exist three times, in Python, Rust and C++, and all three must agree on the same golden vectors.

  1. 01CaptureA TX board broadcasts an ESP-NOW frame 50 times a second. The RX board reads the channel state on every subcarrier, at a rate Saans sets rather than one that depends on other network traffic.
  2. 02Fill the gapsLost packets are interpolated by sequence number, up to one second. Without this, 15% loss made a 15 BPM breather read 17.8 BPM at confidence 0.98.
  3. 03Suppress the roomWalls and furniture dominate the signal. A slowly varying baseline is subtracted from each subcarrier, and each one is weighted by how much it actually moves.
  4. 04FuseA weighted collapse to one signal. Optional BNR selection with sign-flip fusion keeps only the subcarriers that carry breathing.
  5. 05PresenceEnergy in the 0.1–2 Hz band, compared with a threshold learned from 30 s of empty room, with hysteresis so the state doesn't flicker.
  6. 06BreathingBandpass 0.1–0.5 Hz, decimate, then a Goertzel scan of only the breathing band. The peak comes with a confidence calibrated against noise. Below 0.4, the result means “I don't know”.
  7. 07Feature frame68 bytes, twice a second, with a CRC-32. The consent gate on the node writes every vital-sign field as zero unless the space opted in.
  8. 08Space and detectorsThe gateway fuses links (k-of-n presence, best-confidence breathing), runs health and system-health detectors, and logs events that record what was seen and how sure the gateway was.

what it measures

Four signals from one radio link. Each comes with a confidence, and each may abstain.

98.2%occupancy accuracy, one link

Presence, including still people

Motion energy measured against an empty-room calibration. A still person read as empty 2.1% of the time on one link and 0.4% on two.

93.9%within 1 BPM on one link · 100% on three

Breathing rate

A Goertzel scan of the breathing band, 8–24 BPM at rest, with a confidence that abstains instead of guessing. Only in spaces that opted in.

98.7%precision · 81.5–89.9% of pauses caught

Breathing pauses

Presence held, no gross motion, and breathing-band energy below its own baseline for at least 10 s. None were reported in 70.6 h of timelines that had none.

2 Hzhealth report from every link

The system's own health

Rate, loss, calibration age and noise floor. A space with no usable link reports “blind”, never “empty”.

off by default

Heart rate is the weak output. CSI amplitude is nonlinear in chest movement, so breathing harmonics and intermodulation can outshine the cardiac peak. Gated on confidence it is right 97% of the time, but it abstains on 39% of scenarios. It stays off until it has been measured against a reference.

Held-out seeds 2–7: 252 runs, 187 simulated hours, thresholds tuned on other seeds. SYNTHETIC. The simulator's body never sways or blocks line of sight, and real ESP32 noise is unmeasured.

link diversity

One link can go blind. Two rarely go blind at the same time.

sensor topology · not to scale

At 20 MHz an ESP32 sees a narrow slice of spectrum, so a person at the wrong spot on a Fresnel boundary can null every subcarrier at once. No subcarrier trick escapes that: at whole-band blind spots, breathing accuracy stayed near two thirds for every method tested. A second link from another angle rarely shares the same blind spot.

position

Where in the room? Not yet.

We tested radio tomographic imaging on 4–12 simulated single-antenna nodes. In all 15 configurations it lost to a six-line weighted-link-midpoint estimator (0.71 m median error, synthetic), and it answered only 13–18% of the time. Until a real mesh shows it can do better, Saans reports per space, not per position.

what saans is not for

Saying no is part of the design.

  • Identifying anyone, or taking attendanceDevice-free sensing cannot tell people apart. Saans reports spaces, never individuals.
  • DiagnosisNot a medical device. Alerts are advisory and never replace existing procedures.
  • Fall detectionAmplitude-only WiFi cannot see height, so a fall and a sit-down look the same.
  • Tracking people around a roomPosition at this node count lost to a trivial baseline in simulation.
  • Pose, skeletons, multi-person trackingNone of this exists here, and none of it is claimed.
  • Counting crowds across campusExisting access-point analytics count devices per building. Saans works one room at a time.

Privacy is not a setting here. It is a 68-byte message format.

FeatureFrame v1, little-endian, encoded live in this page exactly as spec/wire.md defines it, including the CRC-32. Hover a byte to see its field.

    Raw radio stays on the sensor

    Features leave the node. Raw CSI leaves only in a debug mode, and that mode is flagged in every frame (ADR-001).

    Vitals are opt-in, per space

    Off by default. The consent gate is a single function on the node, short enough to audit in a few minutes. Heart rate needs a second switch.

    No identities, ever

    No names, faces, devices or tracks. Saans reports spaces, not people (ADR-005).

    Roles see less

    Admin, ops, safety and occupancy roles, each optionally limited to certain spaces. Facilities staff see occupied or empty, never vitals. Machines use tokens, people log in, and login is required whenever the API is reachable beyond the gateway itself.

    Health reads are audited

    Every response that contains vital signs or alert evidence is appended to an audit log.

    Retention has an end

    Event logs are deleted after 30 days. The gateway runs on the campus network and calls no cloud.

    Every number is measured, including the bad ones.

    Unit tests passed. The evaluation on scripted timelines still found three bugs. Each fix went into the Python reference first, the golden vectors were regenerated, and those vectors caught the same bug in both the Rust and the C++ ports before either was edited by hand.

    Presence threshold too low

    fixed

    Thirty seconds of an 8 s sliding RMS holds only a few independent windows, so the noise estimate came out far too small. The fix was a floor derived from the filter bandwidth.

    empty room read occupied 36% → 0.6–1.2%

    The motion band leaked breathing

    fixed

    One biquad's skirt passed about 19% of a 0.25 Hz breathing component into the 1–4 Hz motion band. The fix was two cascaded sections.

    still breather vs empty 1.33 / 0.88 → 0.79 / 0.75

    One noisy sample reset every clock

    fixed

    The heartbeat sits in the motion band, so a single sample could restart the stillness timer. The fix was a higher threshold plus three confirming samples.

    false stillness resets 60/h → 0.15/h

    a few questions

    What it sees, what it doesn't, and where the evidence stops.

    Does it use a camera or a microphone at any point?

    No. Sensors read radio amplitude and phase, compute features on the board, and send 68 bytes twice a second. There is no image or audio anywhere in the system that could leak.

    Can it tell who is in a room, or take attendance?

    No, by design. Device-free WiFi sensing cannot tell people apart, and Saans never tries to. It reports spaces, never individuals.

    How accurate is it, really?

    On held-out synthetic timelines, occupancy is 98–99.5% accurate and breathing is within 1 BPM 94–100% of the time, depending on the number of links. Real ESP32 noise, real rooms and real bodies have not been measured yet. That is phase S5, and it needs hardware.

    Is it a medical device?

    No. Breathing and heart outputs are engineering measurements. Alerts are advisory and never replace existing procedures, and each one records what it saw and how confident it was.

    Why is heart rate off?

    CSI amplitude is nonlinear in chest movement, so breathing harmonics and intermodulation products can be stronger than the cardiac peak. Gating on confidence makes the estimate right 97% of the time, but it then abstains on 39% of scenarios. It stays off until it has been measured against a reference.

    Does it detect falls?

    No. A possible-collapse detector exists and caught every simulated fall, and every sit-down too, because amplitude-only WiFi cannot see height. It ships off.

    Does it slow down the WiFi?

    It adds a little airtime. A dedicated TX board broadcasts a 12-byte ESP-NOW frame 50 times a second on one channel, so the sample rate is under Saans's control. It never joins or inspects the network's traffic.

    Does it need the internet?

    No. Sensors talk to a gateway on the campus network, and the API, dashboard and event log are all local. Nothing calls a cloud service.

    Does it work through walls?

    Radio passes through drywall, and a link can span two rooms, but Saans makes no through-wall claim until one has been measured.

    What does it cost?

    The software is MIT-licensed. A room needs two ESP32-S3 boards at about $6–10 each, and a building needs one gateway, such as a Raspberry Pi 5.
